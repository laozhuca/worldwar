Instructions

The war

  • Supply convoys arrive every 20s with men, ammunition and supplies. Captured enemy trenches increase the flow.
  • Machine guns massacre troops in the open. Walking troops under fire get pinned and crawl.
  • Barbed wire slows everyone to a crawl. Shellfire cuts it.
  • Artillery (range 10–36 tiles: nothing closer) kills, cuts wire, damages trenches and suppresses defenders, who can barely shoot back. Shells don't care whose side you're on.
  • Trenches cut casualties sharply. Reinforced trenches (level 2–3) cut them further.

How to win ground

  1. Bombard the enemy wire to cut lanes through it.
  2. Bombard the trench you want so the gunners keep their heads down.
  3. Charge with your rifle squads while the shells are still falling (guns fire every few seconds), so you reach the trench as the barrage lifts. Charging troops can't be pinned, and run much faster than they walk.
  4. Hold the trench, bring up fresh squads, then dig in and wire the new line. Worn-down squads are not refilled: recruit new ones.

In memory of all who served on every side of the Great War, 1914–1918.

1 July 1916

Private Thomas Hale, 19, and Private William Archer, 22, fell in no man's land a few minutes after leaving their trench. They were two of the 19,240 British soldiers killed on the first day of the Battle of the Somme.

Seven days of shelling had not cut the wire, and the machine gunners had waited out the barrage in their deep dugouts. When the guns lifted, they climbed back to their parapets before the attackers could cross.

How to win ground

  • Bombard the wire first, to cut lanes through it.
  • Bombard the machine guns and the trench you want, so their crews keep their heads down.
  • Charge while the last shells are still falling, and reach the trench as the barrage lifts.
  • Then dig in and wire the new line before the counter-attack.

Hale and Archer are fictional; the figures are not.
